About This Work

Alberto Giacometti's Grande Tête Mince belongs to a series of elongated heads created in the early 1950s, when his distinctive style had fully matured. The radical thinness of the neck and the deeply worked bronze surface reflect his existentialist preoccupation with presence and absence.

This exceptional cast was executed in 1954 and bears the Susse Frères foundry mark, Paris — the principal foundry with which Giacometti collaborated throughout his career. The work was acquired directly from the artist's estate and has remained in a single private collection for over four decades.
